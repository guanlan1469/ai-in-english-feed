On October 22, at AGNTCon and MCPCon in San Jose, Google Cloud unveiled Agent Compute Engine, a compute service built just for agents. Every agent gets its own hardware-isolated microVM, a tiny virtual machine with its own kernel and root access.
Alex: The clever part is what is not inside. The sandbox holds no credentials at all. When an agent needs to call a service, its request passes through a gateway outside the microVM, which checks the agent's identity and swaps in the real token in flight. If a prompt injection hijacks the agent, the attacker finds an empty room.
Jordan: It is also built for the way agents actually work: long pauses while they wait on a model or a human. When an agent goes idle, its microVM is checkpointed and paused, then resumed from a snapshot. Google says the service already powers Gemini as a work agent, plus Antigravity and internal training pipelines, with a customer preview coming in the next few months.
